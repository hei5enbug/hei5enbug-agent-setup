# Interviewing discipline

Use this discipline to name a destination and resolve grilling tickets.

## Rules

1. Walk the decision tree one branch at a time.
2. Ask exactly one question, then wait for the human's answer.
3. Attach a recommended answer and a short reason to each question.
4. Resolve prerequisites before decisions that depend on them.
5. Look up facts from the repository, connected tools, or primary sources. Ask the human only for
   judgments and owner decisions.
6. Challenge vague or conflicting answers with a concrete example.
7. Keep an explicit list of decisions made, open decisions, and contradictions.
8. Do not perform the destination work during the interview.

## Naming the destination

Reach a shared answer to these points:

- What artifact, decision, or change marks the end of the map?
- Who will use the result?
- What must be decided before execution can begin?
- What is explicitly outside this effort?
- Which constraints cannot change?

Phrase the destination in one or two lines. Confirm it with the human before creating the map.

## Mapping breadth-first

After the destination is fixed, scan the whole space before going deep. Identify:

- precise questions that can become tickets now;
- dependencies between those questions;
- areas that are in scope but still too vague to phrase as tickets;
- work that lies beyond the destination.

If every question can be resolved in the current session and no meaningful fog remains, stop and
explain that a persistent map would add overhead.

## Closure

A grilling ticket closes only after the human confirms the shared decision. Record the answer, its
reason, and any consequences needed by later tickets.
